*Last updated: November 16, 2026*

I'm Jeffery D. Mitchell, and I run jeff-mitchell.dev, a personal blog about software development. This page explains how the site uses information about visitors. For privacy questions or requests, please use the [contact form](/contact/).

## Hosting and security

This site is hosted through Cloudflare. When you visit, Cloudflare processes connection information, such as your IP address, requested URLs, and browser information, to deliver and help protect the site. Depending on the security features in use, Cloudflare may use security-related cookies. See [Cloudflare's Privacy Policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/) for more information.

## Visitor statistics

I use Plausible Analytics to understand which pages people read and how they find the blog. Plausible processes information such as page URLs, referring sites, approximate location, and browser and device type. According to Plausible, it does not use analytics cookies or persistent visitor identifiers, and it does not store raw IP addresses or raw browser identification strings. See [Plausible's Data Policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy) for details.

## Comments

Comments are provided by Utterances and stored as issues in the site's public GitHub repository. The widget loads automatically on blog posts, so your browser contacts Utterances and GitHub even if you don't comment. They receive connection information, such as your IP address, and information needed to find the discussion for the page.

To comment through the widget, you need to sign in to GitHub and authorize Utterances. **Comments, your GitHub username, and linked profile information are public.** Please don't include information you want to keep private. You can manage your comments on GitHub and revoke Utterances' access in your GitHub account settings. Comments may remain on GitHub until deleted, and copies may remain elsewhere.

See [how Utterances works](https://utteranc.es/) and [GitHub's Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).

## Contact form

When you submit the contact form, your email address and message are sent to Formspree so they can be delivered to me and I can respond. Formspree may also process technical information such as your IP address and browser information. Submissions may be stored in Formspree and in my email inbox until deleted, subject to the providers' retention practices. Please don't send passwords or sensitive personal information.

See [Formspree's Privacy Policy](https://formspree.io/legal/privacy-policy).

## Theme preference

If you change the light or dark theme, the site saves your choice in your browser's local storage so it can remember your preference. You can remove it by clearing this site's stored data in your browser.

## Your choices and rights

You don't need a GitHub account or have to comment to read the blog. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or object to the processing of your personal information, and to complain to a data-protection authority.

Use the [contact form](/contact/) to ask about information I control. Some requests may also need to be directed to the relevant service provider. Providers may process information outside your country; their privacy policies have more information about where and how they process it and any applicable safeguards.
